SIWES Overview

The Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) provided an opportunity to develop practical technical knowledge, gain exposure to an ICT working environment, and strengthen foundational competencies relevant to digital forensics.

Organisation & Department

Host organisation: Gombe State University – ICT Park

Training location: NITDA ICT Park, Gombe State University

Department/Unit: To be confirmed from the official SIWES placement or logbook records.

Training Snapshot

Programme: Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES)

Training period: 24 August 2026 – 13 November 2026

Duration: 12 weeks

Professional field: Digital forensics

Learning Objectives

The training aimed to build a grounding in ICT systems and introduce the principles and practices that support digital forensic examination.

  1. Understand computer hardware architecture, operating systems, and storage technologies.
  2. Develop foundational knowledge of Windows and Linux operating systems.
  3. Understand computer networking principles, protocols, and network communication.
  4. Develop command-line operation and basic system administration skills.
  5. Understand the principles, processes, and methodologies of digital forensics.
  6. Learn how digital evidence is identified, preserved, examined, analysed, and documented.
  7. Understand cryptographic hashing, forensic imaging, evidence integrity, and chain of custody.
  8. Develop foundational Python knowledge and understand its possible forensic applications.
  9. Improve technical documentation, analytical thinking, problem-solving, and communication.
  10. Understand how computer systems and digital technologies support forensic examination.

Responsibilities & Learning Activities

The placement involved structured learning activities and technical exercises covering computer systems, networking, operating systems, and digital forensics.

  • Observed and learned about ICT support operations and technical services.
  • Studied computer hardware components, their functions, and system architecture.
  • Practised basic computer operations and file management.
  • Examined computer specifications and system information.
  • Learned Windows administration and basic Linux operations.
  • Studied networking devices, configurations, and communication protocols.
  • Practised basic Windows Command Prompt and Linux terminal commands.
  • Studied digital forensic principles and potential sources of digital evidence.
  • Learned about evidence preservation, forensic imaging, hashing, and chain of custody.
  • Explored foundational Python concepts and their possible forensic applications.
  • Organised technical notes and documented learning activities.

Weekly Activities & Tasks

Select a week to expand its activities. You can open multiple weeks at the same time.

Week 1 — ICT Orientation and Basic Computer Applications

Activities

  • Introduction to the ICT Park environment and its operations.
  • Introduction to Microsoft Word and document formatting, with practical exercises.
  • Introduction to basic HTML concepts.
  • Discussion of ICT services, technical operations, and professional development.

Focus: ICT orientation, computer applications, and introductory technical exposure.

Week 2 — Revision of Previous Week's Activities

Activities

  • Reviewed Week 1 concepts and practical exercises.
  • Revised Microsoft Word operations and document formatting.
  • Reviewed introductory HTML concepts and the ICT environment.
  • Consolidated previously introduced concepts through revision and practice.

Focus: Reinforcement of foundational ICT knowledge and computer application skills.

Week 3 — Computer Systems Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied the motherboard, CPU, RAM, HDD, PSU, and UPS.
  • Identified computer ports and hardware interfaces.
  • Practised file and folder management and examined file properties and extensions.
  • Studied system information, storage devices, and disk partitions.
  • Examined Task Manager information for CPU, memory, disk, and network use.
  • Reviewed how systems and storage may contain potential digital evidence.

Focus: Computer architecture, storage, system information, and potential evidence sources.

Week 4 — Windows Administration

Activities

  • Studied Windows operating system administration and system configuration.
  • Learned about user accounts and administrator privileges.
  • Studied file and folder permissions, Windows processes, and system services.
  • Examined the relevance of system administration to digital forensic investigations.

Focus: Windows system administration and access control concepts.

Week 5 — Networking Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied LANs, clients, servers, and network devices.
  • Learned about IPv6 addressing and MAC addresses.
  • Examined network configuration information, DNS, and DHCP.
  • Reviewed the functions of routers and switches.
  • Studied TCP/IP, TCP, UDP, and common network ports.
  • Practised basic commands for viewing IP configuration and connection information.

Focus: Network architecture, addressing, protocols, and communication.

Week 6 — Windows and Linux Command-Line Operations

Activities

  • Practised Windows Command Prompt commands for navigation and file management.
  • Reviewed user accounts, privileges, permissions, processes, and services.
  • Introduced Linux command-line operations.
  • Practised pwd, ls, cd, cat, touch, sudo, and mkdir.
  • Reviewed command-line and system information in a forensic context.
  • Participated in activities involving software installation, computer specifications, and networking devices.

Focus: Command-line operations, system administration, and basic operating system management.

Week 7 — Digital Forensics Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied the meaning, purpose, and importance of digital forensics.
  • Reviewed the stages of a digital forensic investigation.
  • Identified potential sources of digital evidence.
  • Studied files, storage devices, system logs, browser records, and metadata as potential evidence sources.
  • Reviewed evidence preservation, integrity, examination, and reporting.

Focus: Digital forensic principles, investigation processes, and evidence sources.

Week 8 — Forensic Evidence and Integrity

Activities

  • Studied digital evidence and the importance of maintaining its integrity.
  • Learned about cryptographic hashing, including MD5 and SHA-256.
  • Studied forensic imaging and the distinction between original storage devices and forensic images.
  • Reviewed chain-of-custody procedures.
  • Studied documentation of evidence identification, collection, handling, storage, and examination.

Focus: Evidence preservation, integrity verification, forensic imaging, and chain of custody.

Week 9 — Introduction to Cybercrime and Case Documentation

Activities

  • Studied introductory concepts of cybercrime and cybercrime investigation.
  • Reviewed common sources of digital evidence and their relevance to incidents.
  • Examined evidence identification and documentation.
  • Reviewed the structure and purpose of investigation case records.
  • Learned about organising investigative information and documenting findings.

Focus: Cybercrime investigation concepts, evidence identification, and case documentation.

Week 10 — Python for Digital Forensics

Activities

  • Reviewed variables, data types, conditions, loops, and basic functions.
  • Examined possible Python applications in digital forensic automation.
  • Reviewed file listing and directory traversal concepts.
  • Studied how Python may support metadata collection, hashing, and text searching.
  • Considered how automation can support repetitive forensic tasks.

Focus: Python fundamentals and introductory forensic automation concepts.

Week 11 — Digital Forensics Practical Investigation

Activities

  • Reviewed the relationship between computer hardware, operating systems, and digital evidence.
  • Studied storage devices and potential evidence sources.
  • Reviewed digital forensic examination concepts.
  • Examined how files, metadata, and system records may support an investigation.
  • Reviewed evidence integrity, documentation, and recording examination activities.

Focus: Forensic examination concepts, evidence analysis, and documentation.

Week 12 — Review, Reporting and Professional Practice

Activities

  • Reviewed hardware, operating systems, storage, files, and system logs as potential sources of forensic information.
  • Reviewed Python automation concepts and possible forensic applications.
  • Practised organising notes and sample examination results into clear records.
  • Reviewed accurate timestamps, evidence identification, and documenting limitations.
  • Summarised technical concepts and skills studied throughout the programme.

Focus: Consolidation of technical knowledge, forensic documentation, reporting, and professional practice.
