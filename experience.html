SIWES Overview

The Student Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES) provided an opportunity to develop practical understanding of ICT operations, computer systems, network support, software environments, and foundational digital-forensics principles.

Learning Objectives

The training aimed to build a grounding in ICT systems and introduce the principles and activities relevant to digital forensics and evidence handling.

Responsibilities & Learning Activities

The placement involved structured learning activities and practical exposure to computer systems, networking, troubleshooting, operating systems, and foundational forensic analysis.

Weekly Activities & Tasks

Select a week to expand its activities. You can open multiple weeks at the same time.

Week 1 — ICT Orientation and Basic Computer Applications

Activities

  • Introduction to the ICT Park environment and workplace operations.
  • Basic computer use, file organisation, and organisational workflows.
Week 2 — Revision of Previous Week's Activities

Activities

  • Reviewed Week 1 concepts and practical exercises.
  • Reinforced understanding of system basics and daily ICT tasks.
Week 3 — Computer Systems Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied the motherboard, CPU, RAM, HDD, PSU, and UPS.
  • Observed how core components work together in a computer system.
Week 4 — Windows Administration

Activities

  • Studied Windows operating system administration and system settings.
  • Practised user, system, and configuration tasks.
Week 5 — Networking Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied LANs, clients, servers, and network devices.
  • Reviewed basic network communication and troubleshooting.
Week 6 — Windows and Linux Command-Line Operations

Activities

  • Practised Windows Command Prompt commands.
  • Reviewed Linux command-line basics and file-system navigation.
Week 7 — Digital Forensics Fundamentals

Activities

  • Studied the meaning, purpose, and importance of digital forensics.
  • Reviewed the relationship between evidence, integrity, and investigation.
Week 8 — Forensic Evidence and Integrity

Activities

  • Studied digital evidence and the importance of maintaining integrity.
  • Reviewed evidence handling principles and documentation requirements.
Week 9 — Introduction to Cybercrime and Case Documentation

Activities

  • Studied introductory concepts of cybercrime.
  • Reviewed case documentation and record keeping.
Week 10 — Python for Digital Forensics

Activities

  • Reviewed variables, data types, conditions, loops, and basic scripting logic.
  • Connected scripting concepts with possible evidence-processing tasks.
Week 11 — Digital Forensics Practical Investigation

Activities

  • Reviewed the relationship between computer systems and forensic examination.
  • Examined how evidence can be preserved and documented for later review.
Week 12 — Review, Reporting and Professional Practice

Activities

  • Reviewed hardware, operating systems, networking, and digital-forensics fundamentals.
  • Discussed professional communication, documentation, and reporting practices.

Challenges & Learning Responses

Computer Hardware & Architecture

Challenge: Understanding the functions and relationships of computer components.

Response: Learned system-level relationships and how hardware supports software operations.

Networking Concepts

Challenge: Understanding addressing, protocols, DNS, DHCP, and network communication.

Response: Studied the basics of network communication and troubleshooting pathways.

Command-Line Operations

Challenge: Becoming familiar with Windows and Linux command-line environments.

Response: Practised basic commands and developed understanding of system navigation and file handling.

Digital Evidence Integrity

Challenge: Understanding how digital evidence can be altered and why integrity matters.

Response: Studied the principle of preserving evidence and maintaining verifiable processing records.

Python & Forensic Applications

Challenge: Connecting programming concepts with possible forensic tasks.

Response: Reviewed scripting and logical processes with potential relevance to data-oriented analysis.

Learning Outcomes

  • Computer systems: Foundational understanding of hardware, software, and basic system relationships.
  • Networking: Awareness of networking communication and troubleshooting fundamentals.
  • Digital forensics: Understanding of evidence handling, integrity, and structured documentation.
  • Professional practice: Improved awareness of technical learning, record-keeping, and professional development.
